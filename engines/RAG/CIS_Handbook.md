## 1. Introduction
Egypt University of Informatics (EUI) was established by the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology (MCIT).
The Faculty of Computing & Information Sciences is one of the faculties of Egypt University of Informatics (EUI) which gives it direct link to the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology (MCIT) projects.

## 2. Program Graduation Requirements
To graduate from the Computing and Information Sciences program, a student must satisfy the following requirements:
- Passing a total of 133 credit hours in at least 6 semesters (Fall and Spring) comprising 4 academic levels of study.
- Achieving a CGPA of at least 2.0 at graduation time.
- To calculate credits remaining for graduation, subtract your achieved credits from 133. For example, if you achieved 58 credit hours, you need 75 credit hours for graduation.

## 2a. Student Level Classification
Student Level is determined according to the total Passed Credit Hours as follows:
- **Freshman**: 0 to 26 passed credit hours
- **Sophomore**: 27 to 59 passed credit hours
- **Junior**: 60 to 93 passed credit hours
- **Senior**: 94 to 133 passed credit hours

## 3. Academic Policies and Absence
A student should satisfy an attendance percentage threshold of **75%** for each course. 
A student shouldn't miss a course exam more than **twice** (either 2 consecutive times or separate).
If a student misses the final exam without an approved excuse, they will receive an "Abs" (Absent from final exam without an approved excuse) grade, which falls under an "F".

## 4. Grading System and GPA
The maximum grade for all courses is 100 points.
Each course has a Letter Grade and a Point Value that are determined according to the Grading Scale table.

### Calculating GPA
To calculate the GPA, divide the total points earned by the total number of credit hours. 
For example, if you got a B in a course of 3 credit hour, your score in it is 9 points (3.0 * 3).

## 5. Course Registration Limits
Students allowed credit hours per semester depend precisely on their CGPA. Here is the breakdown:
- If you have above a 3.0 CGPA, you can register for **21** credit hours.
- If you have between a 2.0 and 3.0 CGPA, you can register for **18** credit hours.
- If you are below 2.0 CGPA, you can register for **15** credit hours.
- If you are below 1.0 CGPA, you can register for **12** credit hours.

**CRITICAL EXCEPTION**: If you are in your final graduating semester, you can override the limit and register for **21** credit hours, even if you are on academic probation!

## 6. Academic Probation and Dismissal
If a student's CGPA drops below 2.0, they are put on academic probation.
If a student wishes to appeal a grade, they must issue a grade appeal within a **week** from grades announcement.

●​ Calculating your GPA and CGPA 
●​ As shown in Table 1: Grading Scale, each letter grade you get in a course corresponds 
to a Point Value you earn. To calculate your Semester Grade points Average (GPA), you 
follow the following steps: 
○​ Multiply the point value of the letter grade by the number of credit hours of the 
course. The result is the points earned for each course. 
○​ Compute the total number of credit hours for the semester (the Sum of all credit 
hours of all courses you registered for this semester). 
○​ Compute the total points you earned for the term (Sum of all points earned in 
step 1 for all courses). 
○​ Divide the total points by the total credit hours. 
○​ The result is the Grade Points Average (GPA) for the semester (results are 
rounded to the nearest hundredth).

●​ Graduating with Honors 
○​ A student in the Computing and Information Sciences Program graduates with an 
“Honors Degree” if he satisfies the following criteria: 
■​ Maintaining a CGPA of at least 3.0 throughout his academic years.
■​ Fulfils his graduation requirements in at most 8 or at least 6 regular 
academic semesters (Fall and Spring). 
■​ Never failed in a course throughout his study in the program (Never got 
an “F” grade). 
■​ Never had any disciplinary penalties during his study in the program.

●​ Program Tracks 
 
○​ The Computing and Information Sciences Program offers 6 specialization tracks 
from which students are allowed to select one of the tracks based on their 
preference, the career path they wish to join, and the open tracks that satisfy the 
required minimal number of students registered. 
○​ A track in brief consists of a set of courses that satisfy the requirements of a 
specialization path. 
○​ Tracks are selected to satisfy the market needs and to bridge the gap between 
the academia and industry. Joining a track entail studying a set of courses that 
together build the attributes of a graduate specialized in this domain. 
○​ All tracks were carefully selected to match with the courses at Minnesota so a 
traveling student can simply continue with his studies without any changes in the 
approach.

academic advisor is required to join the track that matches his capabilities and to

●​ What are office hours? 
○​ Office hours are times that professors (instructors) and teaching assistants 
schedule outside of class to meet with students. 
○​ Office hours are times when you can meet with your professors and teaching 
assistants to discuss the material being presented in class or other related issues 
of interest you might have. You can also meet your academic advisors during 
their office hours.

teaching assistants specially when you have questions about class content, are

exam.
